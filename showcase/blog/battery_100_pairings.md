# 100+ Battery Material Pairings Scored Without Simulation

**How compositional reasoning can screen cathode-electrolyte-anode combinations in seconds**

---

## The Problem

Designing a lithium-ion battery cell means choosing from dozens of cathodes, anodes, and electrolytes. Each combination behaves differently at the interface -- some form stable solid-electrolyte interphases (SEI), others decompose violently. Traditional approaches test these combinations one at a time through expensive electrochemical cycling or computationally intensive DFT simulations.

What if you could score every feasible combination in under a second?

## What We Did

Using KOMPOSOS-III, a compositional reasoning engine built on category theory, we scored **114 battery material pairings** across three interface types:

- **Cathode-Electrolyte**: 6 cathodes x 9 electrolytes (liquid solvents, salts, solid electrolytes)
- **Cathode-Anode**: 6 cathodes x 4 anodes
- **Anode-Electrolyte**: 4 anodes x 9 electrolytes

Each pairing is evaluated across five scoring dimensions:
1. **Ion transport** -- Can lithium ions move efficiently across this interface?
2. **Electrochemical stability** -- Is the electrolyte stable in the electrode's voltage window?
3. **Interface compatibility** -- Will a stable interphase form?
4. **Mechanical compatibility** -- Can the interface survive volume changes during cycling?
5. **Degradation penalty** -- What known failure modes apply?

## Key Findings

### Top Performers: LTO Dominates Compatibility

Lithium titanate (LTO) achieved the highest scores across all electrolyte pairings (0.988). This makes physical sense: LTO is a "zero-strain" anode with negligible volume change during cycling, operates at 1.55V (well within electrolyte stability windows), and forms minimal SEI.

### Bottom Performers: LGPS Voltage Window Problems

Li10GeP2S12 (LGPS) scored poorly against high-voltage cathodes like NMC811, NMC622, and LCO (0.448). LGPS has a narrow electrochemical window (~1.7-2.1V vs Li/Li+) and oxidizes above ~2.5V, making it fundamentally incompatible with 4V-class cathodes without protective coatings.

### The LGPS-Si Failure (0.478)

Silicon anode with LGPS electrolyte scores particularly low because it combines two problems: Si's 300% volume expansion stresses the rigid sulfide electrolyte, and LGPS's limited reduction stability leads to decomposition at Si's low operating potential.

### 109 out of 114 Pairings Are Viable

96% of tested combinations score above the 0.50 viability threshold. The 5 non-viable pairings all involve LGPS with high-voltage or high-strain electrodes -- exactly what the literature predicts.

## How It Works (No Neural Network Required)

KOMPOSOS doesn't train on cycling data. Instead, it reasons about material properties using the structure of a mathematical category:
- **Objects** are materials with validated physical properties (voltage windows, ionic conductivity, CTE, etc.)
- **Morphisms** are compatibility relationships between materials
- **Composition** lets the engine chain A->B and B->C into A->C reasoning

Five independent scorers evaluate each interface. Their weighted composite determines viability. The property data comes from peer-reviewed sources (Materials Project, Goodenough & Park 2013, Xu Chem. Rev. 2004, etc.).

## Why This Matters

This isn't a replacement for electrochemical testing. It's a **screening layer** that answers "which combinations should we test first?" before committing to expensive experiments.

A battery R&D lab testing 114 combinations at the bench would need weeks of electrode fabrication, cell assembly, and cycling. KOMPOSOS generates the full ranking in under a second, letting researchers focus their experimental budget on the most promising candidates.
