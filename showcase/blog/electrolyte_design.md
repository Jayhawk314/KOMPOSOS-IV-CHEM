## Two Scales, One Question

Battery electrolyte design operates at two scales simultaneously:
1. **Molecular scale**: Do the solvent molecules mix well? Does the salt dissolve? Are there unwanted side reactions?
2. **Material scale**: Is the electrolyte stable at the electrode's operating voltage? Does it form a stable SEI? Can it handle the anode's volume expansion?

Most tools handle one scale or the other. Molecular dynamics simulates solvent-solvent interactions. Electrochemical models predict electrode stability windows. Nobody asks both questions at once.

KOMPOSOS-III bridges both scales using a cross-bridge functor that maps molecular properties into material-level compatibility scores.

## Molecular-Level Results

### Best Solvent Pairs

We scored all 21 unique solvent-solvent pairings from 7 common battery solvents:

| Rank | Pair | Score | Notes |
|------|------|-------|-------|
| 1 | EC + FEC | 0.932 | FEC is the fluorinated analog of EC, structurally similar |
| 2 | EC + PC | 0.931 | Both cyclic carbonates, excellent mutual solubility |
| 3 | PC + FEC | 0.931 | Cyclic carbonate family compatibility |
| 4 | DEC + EMC | 0.925 | Both linear carbonates |
| 5 | PC + VC | 0.902 | Both cyclic, VC acts as SEI-forming additive |

The pattern: cyclic carbonates prefer other cyclic carbonates. Linear carbonates (DMC, DEC, EMC) mix well with each other. The standard EC+DMC formulation works because the cyclic-linear combination balances high dielectric constant (EC) with low viscosity (DMC).

### Best Salt-Solvent Combinations

LiPF6 dominates: it scores 0.940 with every solvent tested. This reflects its real-world dominance -- LiPF6 combines adequate conductivity, acceptable thermal stability, and aluminum current collector passivation that no other salt matches across all criteria.

## Cross-Scale Results

### EC+FEC+LiPF6: The Silicon Anode Formulation

This formulation scores 0.932 against every electrode tested. FEC (fluoroethylene carbonate) is the key additive for silicon anodes -- it decomposes preferentially to form a flexible, fluorine-rich SEI that accommodates Si's 300% volume expansion.

### The PC+Graphite Problem

The molecular bridge correctly identifies PC+Graphite as problematic. Propylene carbonate co-intercalates into graphite's layered structure, causing exfoliation and cell failure. This is one of the oldest known failure modes in lithium-ion chemistry (discovered in the 1990s) and our cross-bridge flags it through the chemical veto mechanism.

## How the Cross-Bridge Works

The molecular-material functor operates in four steps:

1. **Molecular compatibility**: Score all solvent-solvent and salt-solvent pairs using 5 molecular scorers (electronic, thermodynamic, steric, solubility, reactivity)
2. **Voltage compatibility**: Check if each molecule's electrochemical window covers the electrode's operating voltage
3. **Thermal compatibility**: Verify that molecules are thermally stable at battery operating temperature
4. **Chemical compatibility**: Check against known bad pairs (PC+Graphite, H2O+Li metal, etc.)

The overall score is the bottleneck (minimum) across all checks -- the system is only as strong as its weakest interface.

## Practical Implications

For electrolyte chemists, this analysis suggests:
- **EC+FEC+LiPF6** is the most universally compatible formulation, particularly for next-gen silicon anodes
- **EC+DMC+LiPF6** remains the gold standard for graphite cells
- Salt choice matters less than solvent choice for molecular compatibility, but matters enormously for material-level effects (Al corrosion, thermal stability)
