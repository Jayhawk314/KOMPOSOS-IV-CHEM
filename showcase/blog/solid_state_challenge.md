## The Multi-Domain Challenge

A solid-state battery isn't just a battery problem. It's simultaneously:
- A **ceramics** problem (LLZO garnet electrolyte must be dense, conductive, and crack-free)
- A **polymer** problem (interlayer coatings must accommodate volume changes)
- A **metal** problem (current collectors must resist corrosion in contact with electrodes)
- A **battery** problem (electrochemistry must work across every interface)

Traditional simulation tools handle one domain at a time. You might run DFT on the cathode-electrolyte interface, then FEA on the mechanical stress, then a separate corrosion model for the collector. Each model has different inputs, different scales, and different assumptions. Nobody runs them together.

## Key Findings

### Every Design Has a Bottleneck

No design scored above 0.50 (the viability threshold). The common culprit: **metal current collector incompatibility** with the cathode operating voltage.

- Cu foil received an electrochemical veto against both LFP and NMC811 -- Cu oxidizes above ~3.4V vs Li/Li+, and both cathodes operate above this.
- Design A (NMC811 + PEO) also received a chemical veto -- PEO degrades above ~3.9V, but NMC811 operates at 4.0-4.3V.

### Synthesis Cost Matters

We also planned synthesis routes for each solid electrolyte:

| Electrolyte | Composite Score | Cost ($/g) | Time (h) | Safety |
|-------------|----------------|------------|----------|--------|
| LLZO | 0.782 | $0.11 | 18.5 | 0.920 |
| Li3PS4 | 0.701 | $0.70 | 10.5 | 0.520 |
| LGPS | 0.498 | $1.50 | 28.5 | 0.240 |

LLZO wins on safety and cost. LGPS is the most expensive and most hazardous (GeS2 and P2S5 precursors are toxic and air-sensitive, requiring glovebox handling).

### The Real Insight: Collectors Need Rethinking

The bottleneck across all designs isn't the exotic solid electrolyte -- it's the mundane current collector. This suggests that solid-state battery R&D should invest more attention in collector compatibility (coated foils, alternative metals, conductive carbon layers) rather than focusing exclusively on electrolyte optimization.

## How the Multi-Domain Analyzer Works

KOMPOSOS uses **cross-domain functors** -- mathematical mappings that translate compatibility between different material domains:

1. `battery_polymer` functor: scores polymer-electrode voltage/thermal/mechanical compatibility
2. `battery_metal` functor: scores collector-electrode electrochemical/galvanic/corrosion compatibility
3. `ceramic_metal` functor: scores ceramic-metal CTE/thermal processing/mechanical compatibility

The analyzer identifies every cross-domain interface, applies the appropriate functor, and reports the **bottleneck** -- the weakest interface that limits the entire system.
