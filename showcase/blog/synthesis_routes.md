## The Manufacturing Question

Knowing *what* materials to combine is only half the battle. The other half: *how do you make them?*

KOMPOSOS-III includes a synthesis planner with 24 curated routes covering cathodes, anodes, solid electrolytes, ceramics, coatings, and polymer films. Each route is scored across four dimensions:

1. **Feasibility** (0.35 weight): Literature-reported success probability for each step
2. **Safety** (0.25 weight): Hazard classification of precursors and process conditions
3. **Cost** (0.20 weight): Precursor costs in USD per gram of product
4. **Time** (0.20 weight): Total process time including calcination, drying, and cooling

## The LFP Story: 3 Routes Compared

LFP (LiFePO4) has the most synthesis routes in our database -- three distinct approaches:

| Route | Composite | Cost | Time | Key Step |
|-------|-----------|------|------|----------|
| Solid-state | 0.852 | $0.10/g | 23h | Calcination at 700C |
| Hydrothermal | 0.814 | $0.08/g | 35h | Autoclave at 180C |
| Sol-gel | 0.790 | $0.12/g | 31h | Gelation + calcination |

Solid-state synthesis wins on composite score -- it's the most mature industrial process. Hydrothermal is cheapest but slowest. Sol-gel offers the finest particle control but at higher cost.

## Cost vs Time Tradeoff

The data reveals a clear tradeoff: the cheapest routes (Al2O3 Bayer at $0.01/g, SiC Acheson at $0.01/g) require the longest processing times (56h and 39h respectively). Fast routes (coatings at 2-3h) cost more per gram but dramatically reduce lab time.

For a research lab optimizing throughput, time-weighted cost ($/g/h) may be more relevant than raw precursor cost.
