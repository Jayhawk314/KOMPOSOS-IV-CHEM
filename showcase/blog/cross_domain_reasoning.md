## The Silo Problem

Materials science is organized by domain: battery researchers talk to battery researchers, ceramics people talk to ceramics people, polymer scientists attend polymer conferences. When a problem spans domains -- like a ceramic-coated battery electrode or a polymer-metal composite -- the experts often don't share a common language, let alone a common simulation tool.

KOMPOSOS-III breaks these silos with cross-domain functors: mathematical mappings that translate compatibility between any two material domains. We ran a systematic sweep to see what happens when you score every feasible cross-domain pairing.

## The Sweep: 146 Cross-Domain Pairings

We tested three cross-domain interfaces:

- **Battery-Polymer**: 8 polymers x 7 battery materials = 56 pairings
- **Battery-Metal**: 5 metals x 6 electrodes x 2 electrolytes = 60 pairings
- **Ceramic-Metal**: 6 ceramics x 5 metals = 30 pairings

## Surprises: What Scored Higher Than Expected

### PVDF Is an Excellent Battery Binder (0.982)

PVDF + NMC811 and PVDF + LFP both scored 0.982 -- the highest cross-domain scores in the entire sweep. This confirms industry practice: PVDF's electrochemical stability, adhesion, and flexibility make it the standard cathode binder. The irony? It's also PFAS, facing potential EU restriction.

### SBR + Graphite Is Equally Good (0.982)

Styrene-butadiene rubber with graphite anode scores identically to PVDF with cathodes. This validates the industry trend toward water-based anode processing using CMC+SBR binders instead of PVDF+NMP.

## Warnings: What Scored Lower Than Expected

### BN Coatings Fail on Every Metal (0.000)

Boron nitride scored zero against all five metals tested. BN's extremely high processing temperature (>2000C for sintering) exceeds the melting point of every substrate metal, making conventional coating impossible. While BN thin films can be deposited by CVD, the standard ceramic-metal coating process fails completely.

### Ceramics on Copper: Universal Failure

Al2O3, ZrO2, TiN, SiC, and Si3N4 all scored zero against copper (Cu_C11000). Copper's low melting point (1085C) and high CTE mismatch with ceramics make it a poor substrate for traditional ceramic coating processes.

## Multi-Domain Designs: The Full Picture

We also tested 6 multi-component designs spanning 2-4 domains:

|--------|-------|--------|------------|
| NMC811 + PVDF + Al | 0.853 | Yes | Al collector (0.835) |

| YSZ on Inconel 718 | 0.832 | Yes | CTE mismatch (0.832) |
| Si + PAN + Cu | 0.736 | Yes | PAN-Si interface (0.722) |
| PEO + LFP + Cu | 0.328 | No | Cu voltage stability |
| Al2O3 + NMC811 + Al | 0.323 | No | Al2O3-Al interface |

### The Viable Designs Are Conventional

The top-scoring multi-domain designs are all established industrial practice: NMC811 with PVDF binder and aluminum collector is the standard lithium-ion cathode architecture. The engine validates what the industry already knows.

### The Non-Viable Designs Reveal Real Problems

PEO + LFP + Cu fails because Cu foil is electrochemically unstable at LFP's operating voltage. Al2O3 coating on Al foil fails because the ceramic-metal interface scores poorly due to CTE mismatch and processing temperature constraints.

## What Cross-Domain Reasoning Reveals

The most valuable output isn't the scores themselves -- it's the **bottleneck identification**. Every multi-domain design has a weakest link, and it's often not the exotic component you'd expect:

- In solid-state batteries, the bottleneck is usually the **current collector**, not the solid electrolyte
- In ceramic coatings, the bottleneck is usually **thermal processing compatibility**, not mechanical properties
- In polymer-battery interfaces, the bottleneck is usually **voltage stability**, not adhesion

This reframes R&D priorities: instead of optimizing the most expensive component, optimize the cheapest one that's actually limiting the system.

```bash

# Or build your own multi-domain query:
python -c "
from cross_bridge.multi_domain import *
analyzer = MultiDomainAnalyzer()
query = MultiDomainQuery(
    name='My design',
    components=[
        MultiDomainComponent(name='NMC811', role='cathode', domain='battery'),
        MultiDomainComponent(name='PVDF', role='binder', domain='polymer'),
        MultiDomainComponent(name='Al_foil', role='collector', domain='metal'),
    ],
    electrolyte='LiPF6',
)
result = analyzer.analyze(query)
print(f'Score: {result.overall_score:.3f}, Viable: {result.viable}')
"
```
